\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript ``Fixed and variable sugar contacts at the subsites of starch-degrading
enzymes: a structural basis for processing functionalised starch,'' by Nedis I. Acevedo-Viloria, Yurimar
Ruiz-Rocha and Aldo F. Combariza, for consideration as a research article in the
\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}.

Functionalised starches, carrying hydroxypropyl, acetyl, octenyl-succinyl or carboxymethyl groups on O2,
O3 or O6 of their glucose units, are processed and analysed with amylolytic enzymes, but where in the
enzyme a substituted glucose unit can sit is not known in general: it has been measured for particular
enzymes and substituents only.

From a database of 637 Protein Data Bank entries of starch-active families, we analysed the density-supported
sugar units at subsites $-3$ to $+3$ of GH13 (79 entries, 29 groups of $\ge$50\% sequence identity) and GH31 (18
entries, 6 groups) and classified every residue within 4.0~\AA{} by kind of contact. Of the 14 positions whose side
chains hydrogen-bond a ring at subsite $-1$ or $+1$, 11 carry one residue type in every group; aliphatic positions
and positions of every kind at the outer subsites vary (two-sided permutation $p = 0.004$). Read oxygen by oxygen
over 114 entries of GH13, GH31, GH14 and GH15, this gives a subsite $\times$ oxygen map of contact states: O2, O3 and
O6 at $-1$ and O3 at $+1$ lie within hydrogen-bonding distance of invariant residues; the GH13 oxygens at $-2$ and
$+2$ next to residues that vary between groups; O6 and the outer subsites are mostly free. Against published
products and kinetics of substituted substrates, reported by state, 12 of the 14 cells predicted not to accept a
substituent agree, and the 7 cells predicted to accept one, or to depend on the enzyme, are compatible; a trivial map
scores 17 of 21 against 19 of 21 for ours, and maps with permuted states agree in 0.59 of their cells. We state
plainly that this evidence rests mainly on phosphorylated glucan, and that no subsite-resolved product data exist
yet for hydroxypropyl, acetyl, octenyl-succinyl or carboxymethyl starch.

We submit to IJBM because the work is a structure--function analysis of proteins that act on a
biological macromolecule, starch. Its practical result is a set of testable hypotheses for those who modify and
digest starch: substitution at O6 should obstruct hydrolysis less than at O2 or O3; the choice of enzyme should
matter at subsites $-2$ and $+2$; and the variable $-2$/$+2$ positions are candidates for engineering enzymes that
accept substituted units.

This manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All
authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The code and data
needed to reproduce every number, table and figure are public at
\url{https://github.com/In-Silico-RG/Starch_Enzyme_Subsites_IJBM}. The use of generative AI in the
preparation of the work is declared in the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
