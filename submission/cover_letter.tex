\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript ``Fixed and variable sugar contacts at the subsites of starch-degrading
enzymes: a structural basis for processing functionalised starch,'' by Nedis I. Acevedo-Viloria, Yurimar
Ruiz-Rocha and Aldo F. Combariza, for consideration as a research article in the
\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}.

Functionalised starches, carrying hydroxypropyl, acetyl, octenyl-succinyl or carboxymethyl groups on O2,
O3 or O6 of their glucose units, are processed and analysed with amylolytic enzymes, but where in the
enzyme a substituted glucose unit can sit is not known in general: it has been measured for particular
enzymes and substituents only.

We assembled 637 Protein Data Bank entries of 14 starch-active CAZy families, assigned every
density-supported sugar unit to its subsite, and classified every residue within 4.0~\AA{} of it as a
side-chain hydrogen bond, an aromatic or an aliphatic contact. In GH13 and GH31, 11 of the 13
hydrogen-bonding positions at subsites $-1$ and $+1$ carry one residue type in every lineage, and
hydrogen-bonding positions are more conserved than aliphatic ones by 0.33 (permutation test,
$p = 0.003$). Read oxygen by oxygen, this gives a subsite $\times$ oxygen map with three states: O2, O3
and O6 at $-1$ and O3 at $+1$ are held by fixed residues; the GH13 oxygens at $-2$ and $+2$ are held by
residues that vary between lineages; O6 and the outer subsites are mostly free. Eight substituents
placed on O2, O3 and O6 of 439 sugar units fit in 80--100\% of the free, exposed cells (phosphate 67\%)
and in 0--38\% of the cells held by fixed residues. Molecular dynamics of wild-type complexes with
glucose-only glucans keep the crystal state in all 10 GH13 cells at $-1$. Against published products and
kinetics of phosphorylated, hydroxyethylated, O-methylated and O-glycosylated substrates, the map agrees
in 19 of 21 subsite--oxygen cells; the two disagreements are both at $+1$.

We submit to IJBM because the work is a structure--function analysis of proteins that act on a
biological macromolecule, starch, and because its result is practical for the journal's readers who
modify and digest starch: substitution at O6 is better tolerated than at O2 or O3; the choice of
enzyme matters at subsites $-2$ and $+2$; and the variable $-2$/$+2$ positions, not the fixed residues
of the catalytic subsite, are the candidates for engineering enzymes that accept substituted units.

This manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All
authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The code and data
needed to reproduce every number, table and figure are public at
\url{https://github.com/In-Silico-RG/Starch_Enzyme_Subsites_IJBM}. The use of generative AI in the
preparation of the work is declared in the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
