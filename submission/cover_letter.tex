\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}

Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript ``A fixed core and a variable rim: hydroxyl contacts at the subsites of $\alpha$-amylases and related starch hydrolases, compared with the hydrolysis of substituted starch,'' by Nedis I. Acevedo-Viloria, Yurimar
Ruiz-Rocha and Aldo F. Combariza, for consideration as a research article in the
\textit{International Journal of Biological Macromolecules}.

Functionalised starches, carrying hydroxypropyl, acetyl, octenyl-succinyl or carboxymethyl groups on O2,
O3 or O6 of their glucose units, are processed and analysed with amylolytic enzymes, but where in the
enzyme a substituted glucose unit can sit is not known in general: it has been measured for particular
enzymes and substituents only.

From a database of 637 Protein Data Bank entries of starch-active families, we analysed the density-supported
sugar units at subsites $-3$ to $+3$ of GH13 (79 entries, 29 groups of $\ge$50\% sequence identity) and GH31 (18
entries, 6 groups) and classified every residue within 4.0~\AA{} by kind of contact. Of the 14 positions whose side
chains hydrogen-bond a ring at subsite $-1$ or $+1$, 11 carry one residue type in every group; the hydrogen-bonding
positions of the outer subsites, and aliphatic positions, vary (two-sided permutation $p = 0.009$ by location).
Read oxygen by oxygen over 114 entries of GH13, GH31, GH14 and GH15, this gives a subsite $\times$ oxygen map of
contact states: O2, O3 and O6 at $-1$ lie within hydrogen-bonding distance of residues conserved in every group in
GH13, GH31 and GH15 (O6 only in GH14), and O3 at $+1$ in all four families; the GH13 oxygens at $-2$ and $+2$ lie next
to residues that vary between groups; O6 and the outer subsites are mostly free. Against published products and
kinetics of substituted substrates, 12 of the 14 cells predicted not to accept a substituent agree. We state plainly
that a trivial map blocking every oxygen at $-1$ and $+1$ does nearly as well (10 of 12 such cells), so the
oxygen-resolved map adds two cells to a subsite-only rule; that this evidence rests mainly on phosphorylated glucan;
and that no subsite-resolved product data exist yet for hydroxypropyl, acetyl, octenyl-succinyl or carboxymethyl
starch. The states are presented as contact states and hypotheses, not as measured tolerances.

We submit to IJBM because the work is a structure--function analysis of proteins that act on a
biological macromolecule, starch. Its practical result is a set of testable hypotheses for those who modify and
digest starch: substitution at O6 should obstruct hydrolysis less than at O2 or O3; the choice of enzyme should
matter at subsites $-2$ and $+2$; and the variable $-2$/$+2$ positions are candidates for engineering enzymes that
accept substituted units.

This manuscript is original, has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. No part of this work has been posted as a preprint. All
authors have approved the submission. The authors declare no competing interests. The code and data
needed to reproduce every number, table and figure are public at
\url{https://github.com/In-Silico-RG/Starch_Enzyme_Subsites_IJBM} (code MIT, data CC BY 4.0). The use of generative AI in the
preparation of the work is declared in the manuscript.

Thank you for considering our manuscript.
